# The shared catalog

The **Catalog** tab is the list of every course anyone using Cairn has added.
It exists so you do not have to be the first person to set up a well-known
course: search for it, and if someone has already imported the playlist and
tidied the entry, enrol in one click.

*"The course catalog grows from the people walking the trail. Anyone can add
more."*

## What is in an entry

A catalog entry is one YouTube playlist, plus the metadata the community has put
on it: course code, title, instructor, year, the ordered lecture list, an
optional link to the course's own website, an optional description, and an
optional [knowledge base](knowledge-bases.md) repo. Entries carry aggregate
[ratings](ratings.md) — usefulness and difficulty — averaged over everyone who
rated them.

Entries with a knowledge base show a link to it, both in the catalog list and on
the course page. That link goes to an ordinary public GitHub repo you can read
without Cairn at all.

## Browsing and enrolling

Browsing and searching the catalog work without an account. So does enrolling:
your enrolment is personal data, and as a guest it is stored in your browser.

The same course is sometimes recorded several times — different years, different
instructors, different uploads. Entries carry a year where it is known, which is
the usual way to tell CS229 from CS229.

## Who can change what

Anyone signed in can add a course. Adding it attributes the entry to your
nickname.

Editing an entry — its title, instructor, course URL, description, or knowledge
base URL — changes it for everyone enrolled, so treat it as editing a shared
document rather than your own copy. Fixing a mangled auto-imported title is a
kindness; overwriting a correct one is not.

Personal fields never live in the catalog. Your progress, your notes and your
ratings are attached to you, not to the shared entry.

## Attribution and deletion

Catalog contributions are public and attributed to a nickname. If you delete
your account, the entries you created stay in the catalog — other people are
enrolled in them — but are reassigned to an anonymous proxy account with no link
to your former identity. See [accounts and sync](accounts-and-sync.md).
