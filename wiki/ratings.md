# Ratings

Cairn rates on two axes, not one, because "was this lecture good?" is the wrong
question when you are deciding what to watch next.

- **Usefulness** (1–5, shown as stars) — how essential is this? Can it be
  skipped?
- **Difficulty** (1–5, shown as a flame) — how hard is it going to be?

A lecture can be essential and brutal, or easy and skippable, and separating the
axes is what makes the numbers actionable. A course whose difficulty average is
4.5 tells you to clear an evening rather than watch it over lunch.

## Rating something

Ratings work at both levels:

- **Per lecture**, from the lecture row on the course page.
- **Per course**, from the course header.

You need an account, and you get one rating per lecture and one per course —
rating again replaces your previous rating rather than adding to it. Your own
rating is shown back to you, so you can see what you gave.

## What everyone sees

Aggregate averages appear on the course header and the lecture rows, for
everyone, including guests. Individual ratings are never attributed: nobody can
see that *you* rated a lecture 2 for usefulness. Only the average moves.

## What they are good for

Newcomers use them the way the ratings are meant to be used: to find the hard
lectures before hitting them, and to spot the ones that can safely be watched at
speed. A course's aggregate difficulty is also a reasonable sanity check on
whether you have the background for it yet.

The [study chat](study-chat.md) does not read ratings — it works from the
lecture, your position, and the course's [knowledge base](knowledge-bases.md).
